# Direction A: Trophy Room

**Concept.** A fumble is a piece of memorabilia. FUMPAD feels like a members' club's trophy case: walnut-dark rooms, bone card stock, brass you only see when you've earned it. Playful copy, serious craft: this is money.

**Palette.** Vault #15100C (page), Leather #201812 (panels), Bone #EEE4D0 (text, card stock), Brass #C9A14A as a foil gradient, used only for claimable value, trophies and levels. Felt #2F6B52 means "crossed 10x". Claret #7A2430 marks sells and the curtain.

**Type.** Big Shoulders Display (Chicago athletic, jersey numerals) for headlines and figures. Cormorant Garamond italic is the engraving voice, so every ticket's "Sold 2.4M $GRUB at 0.0₆41 SOL" reads like a plaque. Hanken Grotesk with tabular numerals runs the ledger. Tiny prices use subscript-zero notation.

**Signature moments.**
- **The ticket.** A perforated stub with a serial number, an engraved line, and a log-scale gauge from 1x to a brass 10x post. When it can be claimed, the stub turns solid brass.
- **The unveiling.** Claret curtains part, the trophy rises, a shine sweeps across it, the amount counts up and brass flakes fall. With reduced motion it appears without animation.
- **The claim card.** A 1200×630 bone certificate with a double frame: "FUMBLED. 12.6X." next to the trophy.

**What makes it ownable.** The mark is a fumbled football whose five laces count the five open tickets a wallet can hold per coin. Brass is scarce on purpose, so when the screen turns gold, it means money you can claim.
