# Direction B: Arcade

**Concept.** FUMPAD's core noun is already arcade vocabulary: a sell prints a *fumble ticket*. This direction takes that literally. Every ticket is a die-cut prize ticket with a perforated stub and a serial number. Progress to 10x is a 10-segment power meter where one segment equals 1x, so "how close am I" reads at a glance. Claiming is cashing in at the prize counter.

**Palette.** Ticket Yellow `#FFC21A` is the hero and the brand. It is reserved for rewards: claimable tickets, the 10x line, the jackpot. Cabinet Ink `#1E1B2E` handles outlines, text and scoreboards. Chalk `#E9EBF2` is the page and white is for data. Fumble Pink `#FF5C8A` only ever means a sell. Green and red only mean price direction. No purple-blue gradients and no glass.

**Type.** Dela Gothic One is the chunky display face for the wordmark and headlines. Schibsted Grotesk handles all UI and money figures. Doto, a dot-matrix face, is limited to LED moments: scoreboards, the jackpot multiplier and ticket serials.

**Signature moments.**
- Tactile buttons have a 4px ink edge and press down physically.
- Stickers act as event tags and achievements.
- The leaderboard is an arcade high-score cabinet.
- The claim moment: the multiplier counts up from 1.0x, the meter fills segment by segment, a "Claim unlocked" stamp slams in and a yellow ticket prints out of a slot. Claiming brings coin-stack confetti and links to the share card.

**Trust.** The game layer stays on the edges. Pool, fee split, queue and prices sit in quiet white panels. Prices use subscript zeros, and every rule is stated plainly.

**Ownable.** The ticket-plus-return-arrow mark, the yellow prize ticket and the 1x-per-segment meter all come straight from the product's own mechanics.
